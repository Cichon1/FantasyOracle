How the rest-of-season projection works

  • This week — the official weekly projection from the Projections tab (on Tuesdays, before the week has been projected, a model-only version of it).
  • Later weeks — games left after this week times a per-game rate: 80% this week's projection and 20% a rest-of-season model. That model uses the weekly model's inputs plus the remaining schedule (games and byes left, how many PPR points the remaining opponents allow to the position), the experts' ROS rank and out / IR flags, and is trained on rest-of-season points with missed games counted as zeros, so injury risk is priced in. Players out this week, on bye, or ruled inactive use the rest-of-season model alone.
  • Official ranking — the model's rank averaged 50/50 with the FantasyPros ROS rank within each position, and the model's point totals handed out in that order, the same rule as the weekly official projection.
  • Range — 10th and 90th percentile models of the per-game rate, scaled per position so about 10% of players finish beyond each end in testing.
  • Honest test — 2020–2025, each season projected at every week by models trained only on earlier seasons, with out-of-sample weekly projections; scored against the FantasyPros ROS snapshot taken the Friday of that week (after Thursday's game, so if anything the experts knew slightly more). The mix share and the 50/50 blend were picked on 2020–22; 2023–25 was scored once at the end. Early in the season (Weeks 4–5) the experts were slightly ahead in the test seasons; from Week 6 on the official ranking led in most weeks.

Refreshed with every scheduled run: Thursday and Sunday after the weekly projections, after each Sunday inactives post, and Tuesday after final results (rest of season then starts at the next week).

A pick's Pts / wk is what the player usually taken at that slot scored per week the next season, in this league's scoring (2020–2025, missed games as zero). Its value (picks have no trade market, so one number) is on the same scale as players' actual value: next season's points above a waiver pickup plus points above the league's last starter, over the weeks of a season that count, times how much next season counts (the chips). The slot comes from simulating the rest of this season 4,000 times (remaining Yahoo matchups, then the 6-team playoff): next season's snake order follows the finish, last place first, so a trade that makes a team better also lowers its own picks' value. Weeks that count: regular-season weeks the team is still alive in, plus playoff weeks it plays in, from the same simulation. Round 16 is the keeper round and counts 0. Players' value as next year's keeper isn't counted.

Accuracy by season (honest walk-forward)

Each season is predicted by models trained only on earlier seasons, using only data that existed at the time (coverage and personnel charting from the previous season, since it's published after a season ends), over every player expected to play: games where a player was on the field without a stat, and players who sat while expected to play, count as zeros. R² is the share of week-to-week variation explained; "played games" leaves out the players who sat. Typical miss is the average absolute error in PPR points.

What's in the model

  • Form & Vegas — weighted and last-3 averages of volume, efficiency and fantasy points; team implied points, spread, total; opponent points allowed to the position.
  • Pace — plays per game, neutral-situation seconds per play and no-huddle rate for both offenses, plays the defense faces, expected plays.
  • Coaching — the head coach's pass rate over expected overall, vs light/heavy boxes and vs base/sub personnel, plus matchup versions.
  • Defensive scheme — blitz, man, zone, Cover 0/1/2/3/4, two-high vs single-high, base/nickel/dime, average linebackers, light/heavy box and pressure rates, each also relative to the league.
  • Defense effectiveness — EPA allowed per dropback in man, zone, two-high, single-high, blitz and non-blitz looks, and per rush.
  • Player vs coverage — each receiver's target share and yards vs man/zone and two-high/single-high, each QB's EPA vs blitz/man/zone/shells, each runner's yards per carry vs box count, weighted by the opponent's tendencies.
  • Offensive style — under-center, play-action, motion, two-back, heavy-personnel and fullback rates; run EPA from under center vs shotgun; play-action EPA with and without a fullback; what the defense allows against play-action and heavy sets, and how often it answers heavy sets with base personnel.
  • Run quality — yards per carry, explosive (10+) and "Gump" (20+) run rates, stuff rate, EPA and success per carry, yards per carry under center vs shotgun; the same allowed by the defense; explosive-runner × two-high and × light-box matchups.
  • Red zone & short yardage — share of team carries inside the 10, share of targets inside the 20, carries inside the 5, short-yardage carries and success, team and defense short-yardage success.
  • Contract, draft & age — current contract as a share of the salary cap, contract year, draft slot, years in the league, age.
  • Receiving usage — aDOT, route participation, targets and yards per route run, first-read target share, deep and end-zone targets, catchable / contested / drop rates.
  • Career history — weekly stats back to 2010: weighted averages at 2-, 8-, 16- and 32-game half-lives; this season to date, last season, two seasons ago; a career average weighted 0.6× per season back; a sample-size blend toward the career (or rookie draft-slot) prior; rookie and new-team flags.
  • Expert consensus — FantasyPros weekly, rest-of-season and preseason positional ranks (snapshots taken before kickoff only).
  • Next Gen Stats — rush yards over expected, separation, cushion, YAC over expected, time to throw, aggressiveness, completion % over expected.
  • Expected fantasy points — opportunity-based expected points, points and TDs over expected (luck), share of team expected points.
  • Depth chart — rank, starter, promoted / demoted since last game. Final injury reports — Out / Doubtful teammates' vacated targets and carries, offensive-line starters out, opposing DB and front-seven starters out.
  • Pass protection — sack, hit and pressure rates allowed vs the opponent's. QB context — QB change, the starter's experience and efficiency, the receiver's history with that QB.
  • Game context — short week, Thursday / prime time, time-zone travel, international, turf, altitude, referee crew penalty rate. Play-caller — the offensive play-caller's pass rate over expected by defensive look, formation tendencies, tenure and change flags (play-caller table compiled for 2018–2026; some seasons medium confidence).
  • Weather — temperature, wind, rain, snow, dome. Usage — offensive snap share. Injuries — the player's practice status, targets and carries vacated by teammates who didn't practice, and whether the starting QB is out.

PPR points: one model per position group (QB, RB, WR/TE), each the equal-weight average of extra-trees LightGBM (10 and 25 games per leaf, 6 seeds each), standard LightGBM (6 seeds) and CatBoost (3 seeds), trained on every game from 2018 through the week before. The official projection averages the model's rank with the FantasyPros weekly rank within each position and hands out the model's own point totals in that order; before the weekly ranking posts (Thursday), the model alone, trained without the weekly-ranking inputs. Ranges from 10th / 90th percentile models; passing, rushing and receiving yards and receptions from one model each. Projected players: on the team's depth chart and either a starter or active in one of its last two games, not ruled Out, not on IR / reserve in that week's roster. Data: nflverse play-by-play, participation and FTN charting, snap counts, injury reports, depth charts, weekly rosters and schedules; ffopportunity expected points; FantasyPros consensus history (DynastyProcess).

Known limits: coverage and personnel charting for the current season isn't published until after it (those inputs hold last season's values, in the backtest too); historical Vegas lines and weather are closing / actual values (removing Vegas lines entirely costs only 0.004 R², so the effect is small); historical injury reports are final Friday reports, so a Thursday run treats a midweek DNP or limited practice as Questionable until the final report posts. Tested and left out (no gain beyond noise): trend features, second-half role, slot proxies, team-first volume, backup-QB interactions, injury type, a rookie combine profile, recency weighting, and removing the expert rank from the model.
